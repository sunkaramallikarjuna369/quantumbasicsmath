Quantum Basis States: |0⟩ and |1⟩

The Foundation of Quantum Computing - Computational Basis States

What are Basis States?

In quantum computing, the computational basis states |0⟩ and |1⟩ are the quantum analogs of classical bits 0 and 1. Unlike classical bits which can only be 0 OR 1, quantum bits (qubits) can exist in a superposition of both states simultaneously. These basis states form a complete orthonormal basis for a single qubit's 2-dimensional Hilbert space.

|1⟩

Vector Representation (Ket Notation)

|0⟩ - The Zero State

[ 1 0 ]

Amplitude of |0⟩: 1
Amplitude of |1⟩: 0

100% probability of measuring 0

|1⟩ - The One State

[ 0 1 ]

Amplitude of |0⟩: 0
Amplitude of |1⟩: 1

100% probability of measuring 1

Bloch Sphere Visualization

The Bloch sphere is a geometric representation of a single qubit state. The |0⟩ state is at the North Pole and |1⟩ is at the South Pole. Any point on the sphere represents a valid qubit state.

Bra Notation (Dual Vectors)

⟨0| - Bra Zero

[ 1   0 ]

The conjugate transpose of |0⟩
⟨0| = |0⟩† = |0⟩*T

⟨1| - Bra One

[ 0   1 ]

The conjugate transpose of |1⟩
⟨1| = |1⟩† = |1⟩*T

Bra-Ket (Bracket) Notation: When we combine a bra and a ket, we get an inner product: ⟨0|0⟩ = 1 (orthonormal), ⟨0|1⟩ = 0 (orthogonal), ⟨1|1⟩ = 1 (orthonormal)

import numpy as np

# |0> and |1>
ket0 = np.array([[1],
                 [0]], dtype=complex)

ket1 = np.array([[0],
                 [1]], dtype=complex)

bra0 = ket0.conj().T
bra1 = ket1.conj().T

Key Concepts

Orthonormality

|0⟩ and |1⟩ are perpendicular (orthogonal) and have unit length (normalized)

Measurement

Measuring a basis state always gives a definite result
