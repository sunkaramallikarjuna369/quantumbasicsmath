Outer Product |φ⟩⟨ψ|

Building Operators from Quantum States

Notation Guide - Read This First!

|0⟩
"Ket zero" - A column vector representing the quantum state "0". Written as: [1, 0]T (T means transpose to column)
⟨0|
"Bra zero" - A row vector, the conjugate transpose of |0⟩. Written as: [1, 0] (a row)
|φ⟩⟨ψ|
"Outer product" - Column vector |φ⟩ times row vector ⟨ψ|. Result is a matrix (2×2 for single qubits).
⟨φ|ψ⟩
"Inner product" - Row vector ⟨φ| times column vector |ψ⟩. Result is a number (scalar).

What Does the Outer Product Do?

Simple Explanation: The outer product |φ⟩⟨ψ| creates an operator (matrix) that:
  1. Checks how much the input overlaps with |ψ⟩
  2. Outputs |φ⟩ scaled by that overlap
(|φ⟩⟨ψ|) |x⟩ = |φ⟩ × ⟨ψ|x⟩

"Apply operator to |x⟩" = "output |φ⟩ times the overlap of ψ with x"

What Does |0⟩⟨1| Actually Do?

The matrix |0⟩⟨1| is an operator. When you apply it to a state, it checks for |1⟩ and outputs |0⟩.

Apply |0⟩⟨1| to |1⟩

(|0⟩⟨1|) × |1⟩ = ?
Step 1: Check overlap: ⟨1|1⟩ = 1
|1⟩ perfectly matches |1⟩
Step 2: Output: 1 × |0⟩ = |0⟩
Result: |0⟩

Transforms |1⟩ into |0⟩

Apply |0⟩⟨1| to |0⟩

(|0⟩⟨1|) × |0⟩ = ?
Step 1: Check overlap: ⟨1|0⟩ = 0
|0⟩ has no overlap with |1⟩
Step 2: Output: 0 × |0⟩ = 0
Result: 0 (destroyed!)

Annihilates |0⟩ completely
