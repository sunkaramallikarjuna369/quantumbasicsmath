Hadamard Gate (H)

The Gateway to Quantum Superposition

What is the Hadamard Gate?

The Hadamard gate is arguably the most important single-qubit gate in quantum computing. It creates an equal superposition from a basis state, putting the qubit into a state where measuring 0 or 1 is equally likely. It's the key to quantum parallelism - the ability to process multiple states simultaneously.

Hadamard Transformations

H|0⟩ = |+⟩

|0⟩
→ H →
|+⟩
H|0⟩ = 1/√2(|0⟩ + |1⟩) = |+⟩

H|1⟩ = |-⟩

|1⟩
→ H →
|-⟩
H|1⟩ = 1/√2(|0⟩ - |1⟩) = |-⟩

H|+⟩ = |0⟩

|+⟩
→ H →
|0⟩

Hadamard is its own inverse: H² = I

H|-⟩ = |1⟩

|-⟩
→ H →
|1⟩

Applying H twice returns to original state

Geometric Interpretation

H is a rotation of π around the axis (X+Z)/√2
It maps Z-axis to X-axis and vice versa

Why Hadamard is Special

Creates Quantum Parallelism

H⊗H⊗...⊗H on n qubits creates superposition of all 2ⁿ states

Key Properties

Self-inverse: H² = I (H = H⁻¹)

Hermitian: H† = H

Unitary: H†H = I

Relation to Paulis: H = (X + Z)/√2

Conjugation: HXH = Z, HZH = X

Hadamard in Deutsch-Jozsa

In the Deutsch-Jozsa algorithm, Hadamard gates are applied at the beginning to create a superposition of all possible inputs, and at the end to interfere the amplitudes. This is the pattern used in many quantum algorithms: Superposition → Oracle → Interference
