Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors

The Special States of Quantum Operators

What are Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors?

An eigenvector |v⟩ of an operator A is a special vector that only gets scaled (not rotated) when A acts on it. The scaling factor is called the eigenvalue λ. In quantum mechanics, eigenvalues represent possible measurement outcomes, and eigenvectors represent the states that give those outcomes with certainty.

A|v⟩ = λ|v⟩    (Eigenvalue Equation)

Eigenvalues of Pauli-Z Gate

The Z gate has eigenvalues +1 and -1, with eigenvectors |0⟩ and |1⟩. This means measuring in the Z-basis (computational basis) gives outcomes 0 or 1, corresponding to eigenvalues +1 and -1.

Eigenvalue: +1

Eigenvector: |0⟩

Z|0⟩ = (+1)|0⟩

Eigenvalue: -1

Eigenvector: |1⟩

Z|1⟩ = (-1)|1⟩

Z Gate Action on |0⟩

Z|0⟩ = |0⟩ (unchanged, λ=+1)

Z Gate Action on |1⟩

Z|1⟩ = -|1⟩ (phase flip, λ=-1)

Physical Meaning

Measurement Outcomes: When you measure an observable (Hermitian operator), you always get one of its eigenvalues.

Definite States: If a system is in an eigenstate of an observable, measuring that observable gives the corresponding eigenvalue with 100% probability.

Superposition: A general state is a superposition of eigenstates. The probability of each outcome is |⟨eigenstate|ψ⟩|².

Unitary Eigenvalues: For unitary operators (quantum gates), all eigenvalues have magnitude 1 (they lie on the unit circle in the complex plane).
